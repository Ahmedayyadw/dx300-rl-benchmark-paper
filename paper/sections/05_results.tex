\label{sec:results}%
All results in this section are simulation results on the single-joint boom
testbed. The geometry of the testbed is estimated and no real-machine data is
used. The numbers therefore rank the controllers on this testbed; they are not
predictions for a machine.

Every controller runs the same 7200 moves: 100 unseen scenarios in each of the
72 test rows of the benchmark protocol. % results/13_benchmark.md, header
Each learned controller is a residual on the balanced PID, trained with the
energy weight $w = 0.03$ and 3 seeds. % results/13_benchmark.md, header
For these controllers we report the mean and the standard deviation over the
seeds. The PID points are deterministic and have no seed spread. The energy
metric is the mechanical energy per move, $E_{\mathrm{mech}}$. The gap to the
floor is the mean $E_{\mathrm{mech}}$ above the mean energy floor of the same
moves, in percent of that floor. The mean floor is 132.5~kJ. % results/README.md, table "Source of every number", row "Energy floor line"; results/12_eval.csv, mean of mean_floor_kJ over the 72 rows = 132.54

\subsection{No learned controller beats the economical PID}

Table~\ref{tab:benchmark} and Fig.~\ref{fig:energy_error} give the main result.
A controller beats the economical PID when it has no more moves over 20~cm tip
error, a lower mean $E_{\mathrm{mech}}$ with $p < 0.05$, and no significantly
larger error. % results/13_benchmark.md, rule above table 13c
The test is a Wilcoxon signed-rank test, paired per move over the 7200 moves
[CITE: Wilcoxon signed-rank test]. % results/13_benchmark.md, text above table 13c
No controller meets this rule. % results/13_benchmark.md, column "beats economical PID" and section "Statement"

The economical PID has no move over 20~cm, a mean tip error of 2.1~cm and
159.1~kJ per move, 20.0~\% above the floor. % results/13_benchmark.md, 13c, row "economical PID"
Every learned controller uses more energy, with $p < 10^{-200}$ in each paired
test. % results/13_benchmark.md, 13c, column "median diff E_mech kJ (p)": largest p of a learned row is 6.3e-208 (BC, 1M)
The learned controllers fall into three clusters.

\emph{Online learners.} SAC is the best online learner, with
$5.7 \pm 1.0$~cm mean error, $1.01 \pm 0.30$~\% of moves over 20~cm and
$191.5 \pm 7.7$~kJ. % results/13_benchmark.md, 13c, row "SAC 100k (Lab 12)"
TD3 uses the least energy of the online learners, $184.3 \pm 3.6$~kJ, but
$25.86 \pm 16.43$~\% of its moves end over 20~cm. % results/13_benchmark.md, 13c, row "TD3 100k"
PPO at the equal budget is the worst controller on both axes of
Fig.~\ref{fig:energy_error}: $14.5 \pm 5.5$~cm and $318.6 \pm 14.8$~kJ. % results/13_benchmark.md, 13c, row "PPO 100k"
With about ten times the environment steps, PPO reaches $7.8 \pm 4.0$~cm and
$216.2 \pm 4.7$~kJ, which is still worse than SAC at the equal budget. % results/13_benchmark.md, 13c, rows "PPO 1M (not equal budget)" and "SAC 100k (Lab 12)"

\emph{Imitating offline learners.} BC and IQL lie between the two PID points in
energy (Fig.~\ref{fig:energy_error}b): $162.6 \pm 1.0$~kJ and
$164.1 \pm 0.4$~kJ, against 159.1~kJ for the economical and 167.5~kJ for the
balanced PID. % results/13_benchmark.md, 13c, rows "BC, 1M transitions", "IQL, 1M transitions", "economical PID", "balanced PID"
BC is the closest learned controller. It has no move over 20~cm and a mean
error of $2.2 \pm 0.2$~cm. % results/13_benchmark.md, 13c, row "BC, 1M transitions" and section "Statement"
It is still worse than the economical PID on both metrics: the median paired
difference is $+0.12$~cm ($p = 5.0 \times 10^{-11}$) and $+1.13$~kJ
($p = 6.3 \times 10^{-208}$). % results/13_benchmark.md, 13c, row "BC, 1M transitions", columns "median diff"
The differences are small, but they have the wrong sign for learning.

\emph{CQL.} CQL does not join BC and IQL. Its mean error is $8.0 \pm 0.6$~cm,
$10.94 \pm 5.54$~\% of its moves end over 20~cm, and its energy,
$190.8 \pm 23.1$~kJ, has the largest seed spread of all controllers. % results/13_benchmark.md, 13c, row "CQL, 1M transitions"; spread: column "E_mech kJ"

The balanced PID shows that the trade between accuracy and energy already
exists inside the classical family. It lowers the mean error from 2.1~cm to
1.4~cm and pays for it with 167.5~kJ instead of 159.1~kJ. % results/13_benchmark.md, 13c, rows "economical PID" and "balanced PID"
No learned controller improves on this trade. In
Fig.~\ref{fig:energy_error}b, BC and IQL both lie above and to the right of
the economical PID: more error and more energy. % results/13_benchmark.csv, rows bc_1M, iql_1M, economical; columns mean_cm_mean, E_mech_mean

The pump-side energy $E_{\mathrm{pump}}$ is available only as a lower bound
that rests on an assumed pump efficiency. % results/13_benchmark.md, "Status of the values"
It puts the nine controllers in the same order as $E_{\mathrm{mech}}$, from
126.5~kJ for the economical PID to $247.9 \pm 12.3$~kJ for PPO at the equal
budget, and is not used further. % results/13_benchmark.md, 13c, column "E_pump kJ (lower bound, ASSUMED)"

\subsection{Energy above the floor}

Fig.~\ref{fig:gap_floor} puts the same result on one axis, the energy above
the floor. The three PID points span 20.0~\% (economical) to 36.0~\% (fast). % results/13_benchmark.md, 13c, row "economical PID"; results/README.md, table "Source of every number", row "Fast PID, 36.0 %"; results/12_eval.csv, rows controller = fast, mean_E_mech_kJ and mean_floor_kJ
BC, with $22.6 \pm 0.7$~\%, and IQL, with $23.8 \pm 0.3$~\%, lie inside this
span. % results/13_benchmark.md, 13c, column "gap to floor %"
Every online learner and CQL lie above it: TD3 $39.0 \pm 2.7$~\%, CQL
$43.9 \pm 17.4$~\%, SAC $44.5 \pm 5.8$~\%, and PPO $140.4 \pm 11.2$~\% at the
equal budget. % results/13_benchmark.md, 13c, column "gap to floor %"
These controllers spend more energy above the floor than the fast PID, the
classical point that was chosen for speed and not for energy. % results/11_real_plant_tuning.md, selection rule of the "fast" point

\subsection{More data does not close the energy gap}

The offline dataset is an equal mix of the balanced and the economical PID
with Gaussian noise on the command ($\sigma = 0.1$). % results/13_benchmark.md, section "Offline dataset"
Fig.~\ref{fig:dataset_size} shows BC and IQL on 100k, 300k and 1M transitions
of it. CQL was trained on 1M transitions only. % results/13_benchmark.md, section "Setup"

More data makes BC more accurate. Its mean error falls from $3.5 \pm 1.5$~cm
to $2.8 \pm 0.9$~cm and $2.2 \pm 0.2$~cm. % results/13_benchmark.md, table "Dataset size (offline)", rows BC
More data does not make BC cheaper: $E_{\mathrm{mech}}$ is $161.7 \pm 1.0$,
$162.7 \pm 0.6$ and $162.6 \pm 1.0$~kJ. % results/13_benchmark.md, table "Dataset size (offline)", rows BC
IQL is flat in accuracy ($2.4 \pm 0.1$, $2.1 \pm 0.1$ and $2.4 \pm 0.5$~cm),
and its energy rises with the dataset, from $162.4 \pm 0.4$ to
$164.1 \pm 0.4$~kJ. % results/13_benchmark.md, table "Dataset size (offline)", rows IQL
IQL on 300k transitions is the only configuration whose error does not differ
significantly from the economical PID (median difference $+0.11$~cm,
$p = 0.072$). It still uses more energy (median $+1.23$~kJ,
$p = 6.0 \times 10^{-221}$). % results/13_benchmark.md, table "Dataset size (offline)", row "IQL, 300k transitions"
No dataset size brings an offline learner down to the 159.1~kJ of the
economical PID. % results/13_benchmark.md, 13c, row "economical PID"
